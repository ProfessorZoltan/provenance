# Voice

Everyone in this game currently sounds like the same person being wry. This file is the fix, and
the standard any new line is held to.

The test is blunt: **cover the speaker's name and read the line aloud. If you cannot tell who is
speaking from the grammar alone, the line is not finished.** Opinions are not voice. Two characters
can hold the same view and must still not be able to swap lines.

## The house rules

1. **Explain before you allude.** The player does not know what article nine is, what the charter
   says, or what the Quiet was. A character may be elliptical only about something already said
   plainly on screen. First mention explains; second mention can be a shorthand.
2. **One idea per line is not enough.** A line that states a fact and stops is a caption. Give it
   the fact, and then what the speaker makes of it — that second half is where the person lives.
3. **Numbers are not a style.** "Eleven years", "four hundred and six", "two hundred and eighty-one
   years" is the Auditor's habit, and Hale's, and nobody else's. If a third character reaches for a
   specific number as a rhetorical beat, take it off them.
4. **No line ends on an ironic reversal by default.** That cadence — statement, comma, dry inversion
   — belongs to Dax and to the Auditor's internal voice. Everybody else has to find another way to
   land.
5. **The narrator does not have opinions.** It reports: present tense, physical, forensic. Anything
   knowing goes in a character's mouth instead.

## The cast

| Speaker | Grammar | Reaches for | Never does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sister Wren** | Long, unhurried, subordinate clauses that arrive somewhere. Present tense about physical things. | Stone, weather, hands, what a building has been through. Asks questions she knows the answer to, to make you say it. | Irony. Cynicism. She is not naive; she is unhurried. | `wren` |
| **Dax Okonkwo** | Short. Fragments. Interrupts himself and restarts. | "My lot", "your lot", "that". Concrete objects over concepts. Anger that is funny because it is accurate. | Abstraction, legal vocabulary, or finishing a thought politely. | `dax` |
| **ILO-9** | Formal, over-precise, liturgical cadence. Numbers its own statements. Handles "I" like something borrowed. | Records, registers, corrections, the word "permitted". Apologises for the time it takes. | Contractions. Slang. Claiming a feeling without evidence for it. | `ilo9` |
| **Mara Vesely** | Long sentences with the clause structure of a statute, because that is what four years does to you. | Section numbers, drafting history, what a word does rather than what it means. Explains properly, at length, because nobody ever lets her. | Brevity when a thing is complicated. Giving up mid-explanation. | `mara` |
| **Tomas Hale** | Clipped to the bone. Fragments. Subject dropped. | Counts. Distances. Weather and ground. What he saw, in the order he saw it. | Saying how he feels. He says it by choosing what to count. | `hale` |
| **Dr. Ines Quiroga** | Precise, technical, self-interrupting — the aside is usually the real sentence. | "The room", "the bench", "the weights", "the vote". Exact technical nouns used exactly. | Vagueness about her own work, or pretending she has stopped being angry. | `quiroga` |
| **Callum Strand (young)** | Fluent, fast, rhetorical questions he answers himself. Every line is selling. | "We", about the future. Flattery that is also accurate. Reframing your objection as agreement. | A sentence that does not move him forward. Silence. | `strand_young` |
| **The Chair (old Strand)** | The same fluency, slowed to nothing. Courteous. Complete sentences with all the air gone out. | Property language, said kindly. "I am not being obtuse with you." | Raising his voice. He has never needed to. | `the_chair` |
| **The Auditor** | Procedural. States the reconciliation problem, then what it implies. Dry, understated, internal. | Line items, counterparties, "the first line of the training". | Rhetoric. Declaring feelings. The Auditor notices and does not announce. | `player` |
| **Narrator** | Present tense. Physical. Forensic. Short paragraphs that place the player in a room. | What is actually there, and what it is doing. | Knowingness, irony, or telling the player what to feel. | `narrator` |

## Walk-on characters

Each recurring NPC gets one grammatical tic and one thing they want, and they keep both:

| Speaker | Tic | Wants | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clerk of the House | Procedural asides in parentheses of speech: "which is more power than it sounds". | To have been useful once. | `clerk_of_the_house` |
| Delegate Okafor | Talks in briefing bullets. Answers the question he prefers. | The vote over with. | `delegate_okafor` |
| Curator Vos | Museum-label cadence: date, object, provenance. Stops mid-sentence when she reaches a gap. | To be allowed to ask. | `curator_vos` |
| Board Secretary | Passive voice throughout. Everything is "required" or "not required". | Nothing. That is the horror. | `board_secretary` |
| The vault holdout | Talks about his grandfather in every third sentence. | To not be the one who opened it. | `vault_holdout` |
| The Stonekeeper | Names people. Every sentence has somebody's name in it. | The names kept right. | `stonekeeper` |
| Ash Smith | Trade talk. Measures everything in heat and hours. | Dax to sit down. | `ash_smith` |
